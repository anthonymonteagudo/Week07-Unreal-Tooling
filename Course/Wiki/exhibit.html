    Create your own museum exhibit

    Make one technical idea observable through a clear comparison, a visitor question, and reproducible evidence.

    On this pageBegin with what the visitor should understandDesign the comparisonExample direction: a readable labelMake it maintainableScope and formal requirements

    Begin with what the visitor should understand

    Complete the sentence: “After using this exhibit, a visitor should be able to explain why…” Choose an idea small enough that the comparison can provide convincing evidence.

    A mirrored lookup, a normal-map shading effect, a material assignment, a coverage threshold, or a source-to-engine relationship can each support a strong exhibit. These are topic possibilities, not mandatory categories.

    Design the comparison

    Design question What it helps you decide
    What stays fixed? Controls that make the comparison interpretable.
    What changes? The variable or relationship being investigated.
    What can a visitor observe? The visible or measurable consequence.
    What should they predict or inspect? The learning action, beyond pressing a button.
    What might they misunderstand? A label, counterexample, or additional view that clarifies the mechanism.

    Use the simplest asset that makes the relationship readable. Complexity should serve the explanation. More advanced work can explore a justified exception or deeper mechanism while preserving a clear beginner entry point.

    Example direction: a readable label

    Keep the image and mesh fixed. Let the visitor compare two coordinate conditions and predict whether the label will read correctly. Show the corresponding sampling relationship and the evidence that supports the explanation.

    This is a suggested concept, not a completed implementation or a claim about a particular course asset. UV lookup explains the mechanism.

    Make it maintainable

    Record the source, relevant asset identities, dependencies, tested settings, verification, and reset procedure. A maintainer should be able to reconstruct the intended comparison after a revision.

    Use a short visitor explanation and a more specific technical record. The documentation examples show why the expected result and recognizable failure matter.

    Scope and formal requirements

    The current instructor direction is to create a museum exhibit. This page develops that teaching/design direction and does not invent a rubric, deadline, group rule, submission format, or required feature list. Use the current Canvas assignment brief for those details.

    Course materials grow through examples, questions, and verified revisions. Use the current Canvas assignment page for deadlines, grading, and submission instructions.
