## Expansion of unfinished decks · confirmed 26 September 2026

- Treat an unfinished slide or section divider as an anchor for additional teaching slides when the intended subject needs development. Replacement copy alone is not always sufficient.
- Preserve the instructor's full stated subject coverage and time blocks. Do not move required topics to optional depth merely because the current deck lacks their content.
- Suggest additions with stable IDs such as 32A, 32B, and 33A, distinct from original slide numbers. Show their insertion points and the proposed live order.
- Map the original outline to live slide/activity destinations and distinguish required introductory coverage from optional deeper derivations. Keep timing and discussion within the confirmed session budget.
- Use supplied historical teaching material and named references as context, with an explicit distinction between sources actually read and source files still unavailable.

## Class questions and discussion · confirmed 9 September 2026

- Plan recurring opportunities for students to ask questions about the specific topic being taught and to discuss it with the room, throughout both lecture and museum activities.
- Put a concise invitation or discussion prompt on the proposed slide itself, plus natural read-aloud wording in its instructor notes. A question present only in notes does not meet this preference.
- Distinguish teacher-posed prediction/check questions from invitations for students' own questions. Include both; do not treat answering a quiz prompt as the only opportunity to ask for clarification.
- Specify where to pause, how much time to reserve, and how the instructor can use the responses. Keep expected answers, timing, and facilitation directions separate from read-aloud narration.
- Budget discussion within the lecture/activity schedule. Protect student investigation and verification time when cutting exposition.
- In museum activities, prompt observation, uncertainty, competing explanations, and the next useful test. Avoid revealing an exhibit's answer before students investigate it.

## Diagram treatment · confirmed 9 September 2026

- Use a transparent canvas, concise labels, strong arrows, and a clear reading route. Keep full sentences and explanatory context in the review HTML and read-aloud notes.
- Incorporate real cropped images of the relevant texture, UV layout, Maya/UE control, mesh, or result. Prefer the same asset across views; label separate examples explicitly. Generic symbols alone are insufficient when real context is available.
- Preserve genuine alpha transparency around the composition while keeping original screenshot pixels intact. Provide editable SVG, transparent PNG, original/crop links, image credits, and intended teaching use.
- Show the previous library diagram → revised asset when changing a prepared visual, separately from the actual current PowerPoint slide → proposal comparison.
