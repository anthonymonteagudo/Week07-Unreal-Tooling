## 01 · Design is purposeful problem solving

Source: [01 Design is Design is Design](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/01%20Design%20is%20Design%20is%20Design.md)

Visual choices communicate priorities, relationships and expectations. Start with the result the user needs; explain why the layout, type, color and spacing support it.

**Source qualification:** The instructor uses graphic design and branding examples to connect visual design to UI. His career examples illustrate a viewpoint, not evidence that one visual treatment works everywhere.

**Teaching adaptation:** For an Unreal tool, define the artist, the selected objects, the intended change and what must remain unchanged before arranging controls.

## 02 · Use references in context

Source: [02 Stop Solving Other People's Problems](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/02%20Stop%20Solving%20Other%20People%27s%20Problems.md)

A polished reference does not reveal its users, constraints or design rationale. Familiar visual cues help people recognize available actions; style alone cannot establish suitability.

**Source qualification:** The transcript argues strongly against seeking inspiration. Adapt this as critical use of references, not a ban on learning from other tools. Avoid carrying its claims about innate design talent into teaching.

**Teaching adaptation:** When showcasing a production tool, ask which problem it solves and which assumptions differ from our classroom tool. Label an action so a new user can predict its effect.

## 03 · Design appearance and behavior together

Source: [03 Form Doesn't and Shouldn't Follow Function](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/03%20Form%20Doesn%27t%20and%20Shouldn%27t%20Follow%20Function.md)

Form and function influence each other. A list of technical requirements does not uniquely determine a usable interface. Visual communication is part of making the operation understandable.

**Source qualification:** The historical architecture/Bauhaus narrative is the lecturer’s framing. It is not independently fact-checked here. Keep it as background rather than a long detour in Week 7.

**Teaching adaptation:** A preview table and an Apply button can use the same underlying operation while communicating very different levels of scope and consequence.

## 04 · Treat principles as questions, not recipes

Source: [04 Balancing Form and Function - Prescription vs Description](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/04%20Balancing%20Form%20and%20Function%20-%20Prescription%20vs%20Description.md)

The lesson distinguishes a description of how designs arise from a prescription that functionality must outrank every other consideration. Judge choices against context and success criteria.

**Source qualification:** The nature/evolution analogy and claims about engineers are rhetorical. Avoid teaching either as a universal account of design or engineering practice.

**Teaching adaptation:** Compare two layouts against the same task: can the artist identify the target, understand the proposed change and find the result? More than one layout may work.

## 05 · Represent the artist’s task

Source: [05 Why Form Follows Function is NOT a UI Design Prescription](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/05%20Why%20Form%20Follows%20Function%20is%20NOT%20a%20UI%20Design%20Prescription.md)

The implementation model is how the system works. The mental model is the user’s understanding. The represented model is what the interface presents. Choose representations that support the user’s decisions.

**Source qualification:** The transcript attributes this distinction to Alan Cooper’s About Face. It argues against automatically creating one button per function or one field per internal input. Expert users may still need technical detail.

**Teaching adaptation:** “VFX duration” can be more useful than separate spawn and kill events. “Destination asset” can be more useful than exposing every internal package operation. Define success by the visible result, not just a successful function return.

## 07 · Simplify without hiding the action

Source: [07 Less is More - Small Screens, Big Challenges](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/07%20Less%20is%20More%20-%20Small%20Screens%2C%20Big%20Challenges.md)

Prioritize what matters now, but preserve labels and cues that explain how to act. Removing interface elements can increase the effort needed to discover a feature.

**Source qualification:** The Apple Watch commentary is historical opinion. The 50-millisecond research claim has no citation in the supplied note. Do not repeat it as a verified measurement. Predictions about the example websites are not observed test results.

**Teaching adaptation:** Keep Preview, Apply, scope and status discoverable. Secondary options can be disclosed when needed, but a sparse panel is not automatically an easier panel.

## 08 · Adapt constraints to the input device

Source: [08 Five Rules for Effective UI Design on Small Screens](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/08%20Five%20Rules%20for%20Effective%20UI%20Design%20on%20Small%20Screens.md)

Five rules in the transcript: consider context; simplify around a primary action; account for thumb reach; provide sufficiently sized and spaced touch targets; minimize unnecessary typing.

**Source qualification:** These are mobile-oriented heuristics. Do not teach “9 mm = 48 pixels” as a universal conversion, or “one primary action” as a rule that forbids complex desktop tools. Click count alone is not a usability measure.

**Teaching adaptation:** For desktop artist tools, investigate mouse precision, keyboard focus, window size, labels and repeated entry. Consider selecting existing assets rather than retyping paths, while keeping the selected destination inspectable.

## 09 · Use spacing to communicate relationships

Source: [09 Balance Creates Visual Order](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/09%20Balance%20Creates%20Visual%20Order.md)

Balance involves positive elements and negative space. Group related information with consistent alignment and internal spacing; use larger separation between groups. Symmetry alone does not establish useful hierarchy.

**Source qualification:** The Smashing Magazine before/after is described in the transcript, but no corresponding image is embedded. Object-count and time-saving claims are not supplied with measured test evidence.

**Teaching adaptation:** Separate target selection, proposed changes, actions and status. Ask a classmate to identify which controls belong together before explaining the layout.

## 10 · Improve hierarchy with a targeted change

Source: [10 Case Studies - Improving Balance in the User Interface](file:///C:/Users/antho/OneDrive/Desktop/Notes/anthony/personal/Duke%20Tech%20Art%20Course%20Notes/17%20UI%20UX%20Design%20Principles/10%20Case%20Studies%20-%20Improving%20Balance%20in%20the%20User%20Interface.md)

The case study adjusts column widths, gutters, padding and separation in a dense news layout. Existing content can become easier to distinguish without rebuilding the entire interface.

**Source qualification:** The embedded Drudge Report comparison is available and retained below. It illustrates a design argument; it does not prove a measured usability improvement.

**Teaching adaptation:** Use the existing course-tool capture for the student-facing example. Change spacing or grouping as one testable variable; observe whether a fresh tester identifies the target and next action more reliably.
