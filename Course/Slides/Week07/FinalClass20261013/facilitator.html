Lab 1: finalized concept and facilitation

Purpose: establish a shared way to judge a tool before teaching UX terminology. The batch-renaming case isolates scope and output correctness without requiring setup, code knowledge, or criticism of a student’s work. Its invented failures contrast with safeguards visible in the instructor’s current tool.

Opening script: “Decide what should happen before I show the result. You are reviewing behavior. Name the evidence that would make you trust it.”

4:52–4:53: distribute sheet, assign reader/recorder/evidence checker/reporter and clarify the rule. 4:53–4:54: individual prediction. 4:54–4:56: group comparison and expected names. 4:56–4:57: record initial trust criterion. 4:57–4:58: reveal and inspect. 4:58–4:59: finding and retest. 4:59–5:00: two 20-second reports and 20 seconds for student questions.

Instructor key

Expected: SM_crate_A, SM_crate_B, SM_crate_C, barrel_D. Observed: the first two match, the third gets a doubled prefix, and the unselected barrel changes. Prioritize unintended scope because it changes unrelated work, while accepting another reasoned priority. The success count is insufficient. Undo, a second run, performance and the internal cause are unknown.

Model finding: “With only the three crates selected, I expected barrel_D to stay unchanged, but it became SM_barrel_D. This changes work outside the requested scope.” Retest: restore all four original names, select only the crates, run once, then verify all four expected names. A second-run test is a useful proposed extension, not something observed in the case.

During UX, ask which interface evidence could expose the scope error before Apply. At 5:16, students annotate rather than erase their first judgment. Student output is one group sheet with expected/observed results, a supported finding, and a retest.

Lab 2: setup and staff positions

Form groups of four and assign A–D before the briefing. For 24 students, print six Lab 1 sheets, 24 preparation cards, 72 peer sheets, and 24 reflection sheets, plus spares. Label six groups and display a timer. Materials stay with authors. Existing course policy governs any submission; these activities create no new grading or deadline rules.

The instructor calls time and handles blockers. If available, co-teacher circulates through groups 1–3 and TA through 4–6. With one staff member, visit each group during its reset window. Check that both directions of review occurred and each author has a sheet. Do not solve the tool for the tester.

Briefing script, 5:20–5:23

“Each of you will test three classmates’ tools and receive three reviews. Give a goal and prepared inputs, then let your partner attempt it. Reviewers, say what you expect and record what happens. Authors, observe before coaching and record any help. Use your prepared machine. If execution is unavailable, declare recorded or concept review. What questions do you have about the roles?”

Show the round schedule. Later letter tests earlier letter’s tool first. At minute four, swap. At minute eight, finish and reset. Spend the final 45 seconds confirming A–D and readiness. Everyone needs the same bounded primary task for all rounds.

Interventions that preserve evidence

  • Author starts teaching clicks: “Let them attempt the goal first. Record the help if it is needed.”
  • Feedback is vague: “Which action, expected result and visible observation support that?”
  • Tester is silent: “What do you expect this action to do?” Do not imply the correct action.
  • Tool fails to launch: allow at most one minute of the attempt, mark blocked, then use the declared fallback. Do not install software during rounds.
  • Risk to real files or assets: stop the action immediately. Use a copy, recording or paper mode.
  • No fault observed: keep an evidenced strength and propose another test. Do not invent a defect.
  • Feedback concerns personal taste: connect it to task completion or label it a preference.

Unfinished work and attendance

Runnable slice: test only its stated scope. Recording: pause for predictions and label recorded evidence. Screenshots: review visible states only. Paper/concept: reviewer points to intended actions while author supplies explicitly imagined responses. No artifact: use the preparation card to sketch an interface. All students still act as reviewers. Concept feedback does not establish runtime success.

Attendance procedure: form as many groups of four as possible. If one person remains, make one group of five from them and a quartet. Pair round 1 A–B/C–D (E observes), round 2 A–C/B–E (D observes), round 3 A–D/C–E (B observes). A and C receive three peer reviews; B, D and E receive two. Label the gap rather than claiming three. Use staff feedback only if staffing and time allow.

If two remain and at least six are present, convert a quartet plus the pair into two trios. Each trio uses A–B, A–C, B–C with the third member observing each round. Each student receives two distinct student reviews. Three remaining use the same trio route. A group of two exchanges one reciprocal review, then uses remaining rounds for retesting labeled as repeat evidence. For 23 students, five quartets and one trio work without staff participation; an available staff member may fill D for the trio, clearly labeled as staff review. Do not count an observer or repeat partner as an additional independent tester.

Overrun and synthesis

Protect the break, four-minute preparation and three full attempts. Use the existing question/recovery reserves first. Keep optional depth hidden. If demo setup stalls, show prepared captures within its slot. End the UX explanation at 5:16. If an unavoidable late start reaches peer testing, state the reduced number of complete rounds honestly and retain five minutes of synthesis; do not claim that rushed or missing reviews occurred.

5:55–5:57 read reviews. 5:57–5:59 choose one issue/uncertainty, consequence, revision and retest. 5:59–6:00 record changed judgment and remaining question. Quiet writing replaces a full-room report. Staff look for a concrete input, action, observable outcome and protected scope.
