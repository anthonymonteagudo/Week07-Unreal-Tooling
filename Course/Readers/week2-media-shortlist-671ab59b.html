Week 2: media shortlist and incorporation plan

Prepared 8 September 2026. Anchors refer to the current 141-slide lab-aligned Week 2 deck, including hidden positions. This shortlist is a selection pool, not a recommendation to show everything. Existing third-party resources and proposed recordings of the teaching project are listed separately. No media has been inserted into the PowerPoint.

The smallest useful selection.

Use six visual teaching moments: a UV lookup, a normal-direction comparison, a roughness change, an alpha/blend comparison, a Maya-to-UE handoff, and one skeletal bend. Make color/data interpretation part of the roughness investigation. Mip shimmer deserves a short comparison if that extension will be demonstrated. Book of Shaders and the research comparisons can remain linked exploration.

Select a still when students must compare identities or settings, a short loop when they must perceive change, and a paused demonstration when they must predict an outcome. Aim for roughly 15–45 seconds of motion per concept, with discussion separately budgeted. These are proposed teaching durations, not measured source-video lengths unless stated below.

C01: Maya → UE → revision → reimport Use one simple mesh with two material regions. Show Maya face membership and a file-texture connection; show corresponding UE slots and material/instance assignment; revise one source relationship, reimport, and inspect the result. Three separate 15–25-second segments, with pauses “Which relationship owns the failure?” Keep source identity and expected slot mapping visible. Confirm the demonstrated importer behavior in the actual project.
C02: EX23 color versus numerical data Start with fixed mesh, camera, light, Base Color, and numerical RGB roughness map. Show the role and relevant UE texture/sampler settings; compare the one intended interpretation change. 15–25 seconds “What does this number mean?” Finish with expected/observed/result fields, ready to become a Week 3 read-only check. If compression or sampler settings also change, document them rather than calling it a one-variable comparison.
C03: EX24 normal direction Compare normal input absent/present, then demonstrate the exhibit’s specific import/convention fault separately. Move the light through two directions. Include a wireframe or outline check. 20–30 seconds “Did geometry change, or did the lighting response change?” Do not combine a green-channel fault, wrong sampler, and incorrect compression into one unexplained repair.
C04: Alpha Leaf Use the same texture and planar geometry in three prepared materials: Opaque, Masked, Translucent. Show alpha separately. For the threshold comparison, change only the mask cutoff. 15–25 seconds, or three stills “Does storing alpha make an object transparent?” Avoid implying clipped pixels remove triangles or collision. Keep the Two Sided test separate.
C05: EX29 UV lookup Show a directional checker/label in Maya and UE. Rotate or mirror one UV shell, then compare; separately demonstrate a wrong material UV-channel selection if needed. 15–20 seconds “Wrong image, wrong assignment, or wrong coordinates?” Keep image content and intended density controlled.
C06: mip shimmer / LOD distinction Record a fixed camera path over a detailed texture with two prepared mip configurations; then use a separate mesh example to show the active mesh LOD and statistics. 15–20 seconds per comparison “Did the representation of the texture or the mesh change?” Keep streaming residency controlled enough to interpret the mip comparison.
C07: one skeletal bend Start with one simple limb and two influences. Show joint motion, weight colors, the surface result, then the matching UE motion. Add one controlled defect only after the correct case is clear. 20–30 seconds “Wrong pose, wrong following, or wrong playback?” The manifest’s skeletal exhibits are display-ready, so verify interactive controls before promising student repair.

If time only permits three new captures, prioritize C01, C02, and C03. Use the existing external sources for alpha and skeletal orientation. This puts the limited preparation time into your distinctive Maya/UE handoff and the two documented texture-interpretation stations.

Make media readable in the room and useful afterward.

  • State the question before playing. Label the changed variable beside its control and the effect beside the object.
  • Keep camera, lighting, geometry, and unrelated inputs fixed whenever the comparison depends on them. Show any intentional exception explicitly.
  • Freeze the decisive result. The student PDF needs the before/after state and a caption, not an empty video poster or a link with no explanatory context.
  • Show only one moving illustration at a time. Pause it while explaining an unrelated relationship.
  • Use labels in addition to color, especially for direction axes, alpha states, and correct/incorrect comparisons.
  • Credit the creator, source page, figure or chapter, and software/version context. Keep the original resource linked in the study notes.
  • Test the final embedded media in PowerPoint Slide Show and on the classroom display. The browser checks above verify selected source media, not PowerPoint embedding, offline availability, or classroom playback.

Suggested recurring caption: Prediction → Change → Observation → Explanation → Lab check. For the assignment, students can use that same structure to design their museum exhibit. For Week 3, the last step becomes the explicit input, rule, and report that a Python script can implement.
