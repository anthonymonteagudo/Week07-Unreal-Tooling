Review format preferences

Section clarity and visible API teaching · confirmed 13 October 2026

  • Use clear title transition slides between teaching sections, with distinct section colors and explicit section names. Maintain segmented, categorized sequencing.
  • Put substantive Unreal Python API explanations and readable code directly on the slides, tied to the current demos and course materials.
  • Keep a continuous live teaching route. Group optional reference material after the live sequence, preserving stable review anchors in comparisons.

Original deck styling and preparation reviews · confirmed 30 September 2026

  • The instructor did not approve the visual overhaul to the Week 2 deck and prefers its original appearance. Treat the original/source deck styling as the baseline. Sensible ordering changes do not imply approval of a new visual theme.
  • For the Week 5 follow-up, provide a comprehensive HTML preparation and adaptation review. Include dedicated strong sources, specific reading guidance, original slide anchors, teaching notes, and direct links to visual exports and editable source files. Clearly identify raster-only exports and related alternatives that are not the exact original master.
  • Keep content/order proposals separate from visual redesign proposals. The prior restyled Week 5 deliverable is a historical draft, not the approved visual template. Do not change slide decks when the requested deliverable is the review page.

Expansion of unfinished decks · confirmed 26 September 2026

  • Treat an unfinished slide or section divider as an anchor for additional teaching slides when the intended subject needs development. Replacement copy alone is not always sufficient.
  • Preserve the instructor's full stated subject coverage and time blocks. Do not move required topics to optional depth merely because the current deck lacks their content.
  • Suggest additions with stable IDs such as 32A, 32B, and 33A, distinct from original slide numbers. Show their insertion points and the proposed live order.
  • Map the original outline to live slide/activity destinations and distinguish required introductory coverage from optional deeper derivations. Keep timing and discussion within the confirmed session budget.
  • Use supplied historical teaching material and named references as context, with an explicit distinction between sources actually read and source files still unavailable.

Confirmed by the instructor on 8 September 2026 for future course-material reviews.

  • Use HTML as the main review format, with search, current slide numbers, and a filter for the proposed live route. A Markdown copy is useful for copying text.
  • Show current content → proposed content. Include the actual current slide image and faithfully extracted text where available. Do not substitute a summary for the original wording without labeling it.
  • State edits at the field level. Distinguish keeping a slide/topic from keeping its exact title, bullets, or visual. Use explicit instructions such as “keep this title,” “replace the bullet block,” or “merge into slide 14 and remove this slide from the live route.”
  • Keep the current slide numbers as stable review anchors. Link merge destinations and show proposed presentation order separately.
  • Write proposed instructor notes as a script the instructor can read aloud to the class. Address students directly, explain the concept, and include natural prediction or discussion questions.
  • Keep editing directions, preparation tasks, timing guidance, and source-verification caveats outside the read-aloud script.
  • Distinguish existing media from proposed recordings. Keep source citations and image attribution explicit.
  • For merged/reference slides, state whether the proposed wording is an optional study version rather than a required additional live slide.
  • Propose a concrete slide layout and visual treatment, with usable links to candidate images, clips, figures, or existing course assets. Keep visual-reference links separate from conceptual citations.
  • Add a short teaching rationale explaining why the proposed treatment supports understanding, retrieval, or transfer to the lab. This is editorial judgment, separate from the read-aloud script.
  • Keep the review iterative and retain revision history. Store teaching/review materials under Course/Slides, outside the Unreal integration project, with an adjacent Course/Wiki that grows week by week.

Class questions and discussion · confirmed 9 September 2026

  • Plan recurring opportunities for students to ask questions about the specific topic being taught and to discuss it with the room, throughout both lecture and museum activities.
  • Put a concise invitation or discussion prompt on the proposed slide itself, plus natural read-aloud wording in its instructor notes. A question present only in notes does not meet this preference.
  • Distinguish teacher-posed prediction/check questions from invitations for students' own questions. Include both; do not treat answering a quiz prompt as the only opportunity to ask for clarification.
  • Specify where to pause, how much time to reserve, and how the instructor can use the responses. Keep expected answers, timing, and facilitation directions separate from read-aloud narration.
  • Budget discussion within the lecture/activity schedule. Protect student investigation and verification time when cutting exposition.
  • In museum activities, prompt observation, uncertainty, competing explanations, and the next useful test. Avoid revealing an exhibit's answer before students investigate it.

Artistic intent, optional depth, and reusable visuals · 9 September 2026

  • Reintroduce artistic judgment through a concise live concept and concrete asset example, then connect it to the technical inspection. Keep the detailed art survey available for independent study.
  • Add named Explore further pointers on slides and in read-aloud notes. Organize useful study material as an appendix with a clear entry question, relevant source slides, a small exploration, and a return link. Keep unfinished or obsolete cuts in the instructor archive. Do not move required lab prerequisites into optional reading.
  • Preserve visual continuity with Week 1 through the four existing lens badges, palette, typography references, and stable meanings. Use the course visual library for reusable icons and diagrams; add explicit intended use and direct PNG/SVG links in the review.
  • Prepared documents should open as readable local HTML pages with headings, images, navigation, and print support. Offer editable Markdown/CSV as a separate source link. External primary sources remain labeled references, not substitutes for prepared handouts.

Diagram treatment · confirmed 9 September 2026

  • Use a transparent canvas, concise labels, strong arrows, and a clear reading route. Keep full sentences and explanatory context in the review HTML and read-aloud notes.
  • Incorporate real cropped images of the relevant texture, UV layout, Maya/UE control, mesh, or result. Prefer the same asset across views; label separate examples explicitly. Generic symbols alone are insufficient when real context is available.
  • Preserve genuine alpha transparency around the composition while keeping original screenshot pixels intact. Provide editable SVG, transparent PNG, original/crop links, image credits, and intended teaching use.
  • Show the previous library diagram → revised asset when changing a prepared visual, separately from the actual current PowerPoint slide → proposal comparison.

Content economy and application bridges · confirmed 30 September 2026

  • Prioritize concise, accurate slide text and remove superfluous or repeated content. Preserve useful material in notes or optional references.
  • Ground technical-art math in short real-world game-development or GDC application slides between concepts. Provide strong primary sources, exact reading pointers, and distinguish documented production practice from illustrative classroom math.
  • Base content reviews on the latest supplied WIP, retain current slide-number anchors, and propose sequencing separately from visual styling.
