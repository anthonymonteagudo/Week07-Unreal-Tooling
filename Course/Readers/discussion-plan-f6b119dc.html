The break and Week 1 recap retain their proposed durations. Rehearse this allocation: it is a planning estimate, not a claim that all groups or questions will fit it. If a prerequisite is unclear, shorten the next optional explanation. Protect investigation and verification rather than compressing the pause to a token invitation.

Two different opportunities matter. A prediction such as “Which way will the vertex move?” lets students reason with an example. An invitation such as “What questions do you have about the joint-to-vertex relationship?” lets students identify what they need explained. Include both. Slides 62 and 100 provide dedicated invitations after complicated relationships have been introduced.

Keep the topic visible while opening the room. Place one brief prompt beside the relevant image or settings view, or temporarily replace the concluding bullets with it. Do not shrink the existing content to fit another paragraph. Reveal results after prediction questions. The expected answer and facilitation notes belong only in instructor guidance. The on-slide prompt must remain useful when students later review the deck.

Leave space to think and respond. The suggested 8–10 seconds of quiet thinking is a practical cue, not a universal threshold. Use pointing, a brief peer exchange, or a question about a particular step so speaking does not require advanced vocabulary. Hear a different explanation before revealing the answer. Silence does not establish understanding. A student may pass; do not turn an invitation into a public test of an individual.

Let answers change the next step. If a response exposes a prerequisite misunderstanding, return to the same visual and resolve it before adding detail. If students disagree, ask what observation would distinguish the explanations. Answer relevant student questions in the moment where possible. For a question that needs investigation, record the question and the asset, evidence, or source needed to answer it; do not improvise a diagnosis or promise a result.

Keep museum questions visible across the lecture. Add a small “Question raised” field to the existing observation record. At slide 75, select one question to carry into the relevant anatomy section and explicitly return to it. During repair, circulate with “What changed your explanation?” and “What would you inspect next?” Use slide 127's return visit to share evidence and uncertainty. This adds a discussion habit, not a new graded submission or an advance answer key.
