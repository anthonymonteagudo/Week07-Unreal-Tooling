Week 2: a shorter technical-documentation section

Recommendation based on the current lab-aligned deck, slides 112–120 and 128–132, reviewed 8 September 2026. This document proposes replacement content. It does not modify the PowerPoint or establish new grading/submission requirements.

Replace nine lecture slides with four, taught in roughly six minutes. Keep one lab submission slide.

The existing section repeatedly states useful principles without showing a completed exhibit record. The repetition continues in the lab briefing. Students encounter reading sources, reading efficiently, testing claims, writing notes, document types, leverage, required fields, a suggested structure, and quality criteria as separate topics.

Teach one recognizable job instead: another person can open, explain, verify, and reset your exhibit using the documentation. Let the examples demonstrate the writing principles. The familiar asset problem supplies the technical context, so the documentation section does not have to teach another material concept.

Proposed slide sequence

Slide On-slide content Visual and delivery Time
1. Documentation for your exhibit “A visitor needs to know what to observe. A maintainer needs to know how the exhibit works and how to reproduce it.” Closing line: “A useful record lets someone continue without its author.” Show a brief visitor caption beside a small excerpt from its maintenance notes. Explain that these can be sections of the same document. Keep diagnosis/solution details out of the visitor prompt when discovery is part of the exhibit. 45 sec
2. A documented test Three callouts: “Expected result”; “Evidence of a failure”; “Limits of the test.” Show selected portions of the Khronos example below. Ask students to identify the detail that lets a reader judge the result. Reveal the relevant caption after their prediction. 75 sec
3. An exhibit record Use the compact teaching example below, with the identity/header and four short blocks. Contrast the one-line weak note with the stronger record. Enlarge the evidence and reset blocks during explanation. Link the full record rather than shrinking a whole document to unreadable text. 2 min
4. The handoff check “Can a teammate locate the assets, reproduce the comparison, judge the result, and restore the starting state?” Footer: “Record what you verified and what remains uncertain.” Give students 45 seconds to identify one missing detail in the weak note. Show how an explicit expected state becomes a candidate Week 3 check. The actual peer handoff can happen during lab work. 90 sec

Allow approximately 30 seconds for transitions. These are proposed teaching timings. The first three slides establish purpose, show a real example, and model the assignment document. The fourth checks whether students can use the idea.

Compact worked example for slide 3

The following is a constructed teaching case, inspired by the EX29 material-side UV mirror. Its asset names, settings, and outcomes are illustrative. It is not a claim about the current EX29 runtime graph or a completed project test. In class, replace it with the record from the demonstrated repair when that evidence is available.

Weak note:

The texture was backwards. I flipped it and it works now.

Stronger record:

UV Mirror demonstration
Teaching scene: L_Doc_UV · UE 5.7 · Owner: example student
Panel: SM_DocPanel · Materials: M_Doc_Reference, M_Doc_Mirror

Purpose and observation
Compare the labeled panels from the marked front view. Both use the same mesh and image. Only the demonstration panel reads backward.

Cause and change
Its material changes U to 1 − U before sampling. Bypassing that operation restores the label’s orientation. The image and Maya UVs remain unchanged.

Verification
Repeat the front-view comparison. Confirm readable text and unchanged pattern size. The example’s successful result requires both the visual comparison and the material connection to agree.

Reset and limits
Restore the U-inversion connection to reproduce the mirrored state. This demonstration covers material sampling, not missing source UVs. Test again after changing the parent material or mesh UV layout.

For the actual submitted record, link the full project/asset paths and the relevant source revision. Attach a labeled comparison and the inspected setting or connection. Record the tested software/project version, date, and actual outcome. The short example demonstrates the writing, but its illustrative outcomes and missing project evidence must not be mistaken for a verified submission.

Make the difference between the two notes explicit: the stronger one identifies the responsible relationship, a repeatable action, evidence criteria, and a reset. It explains why the change affects the result. The weak note leaves the next person to rediscover all of that.

A starter structure that supports both lab and assignment

Use one concise main record with linked evidence. Length should follow the complexity of the dependencies, not a word-count target.

Part Questions it answers
Identity and purpose Which exhibit, map, assets, source files, and revision? Who maintains it? What should it teach, and why does that behavior matter?
Starting state and comparison How does someone open and reproduce it? What should the visitor observe? What stays fixed, and what varies?
Explanation and evidence Which data or setting causes the behavior? Where is it authored? What supports the explanation? Include the repair if this is a fault investigation.
Verification and maintenance What did you actually test, with what result? How do you reset it? What requires rechecking after a source edit/reimport? What remains uncertain?
References Where did the assets or technical explanation come from? Link the relevant source and attribution.

The lab fills this record through an investigation. The exhibit assignment uses it to explain and maintain a deliberately constructed demonstration. For a comparison of valid artistic choices, describe the behavior or tradeoff without labeling one choice a defect. Any formal assignment requirement for a controlled fault still belongs in the definitive assignment brief.

Keep a short visitor-facing prompt at the beginning. Put the technical explanation and solution in the maintenance portion when the exhibit asks visitors to investigate first. This is an organization suggestion, not a request for additional separate deliverables.

Week 3 connection

Use one line at the end: “An expected state that we can inspect becomes a candidate for an automated check.” Then ask which part of the record requires artistic judgment and which part a script can query. A Python report can check a declared UV channel or texture interpretation. It cannot infer the intended appearance merely because an asset looks unusual.

The success criterion for this section is observable: a student can identify what is missing from a vague note and improve it enough for another person to reproduce the exhibit. A list of writing virtues does not establish that skill.
