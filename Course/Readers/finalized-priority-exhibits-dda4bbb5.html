Shared implementation contract

Every station uses one shared exhibit actor and a data record. Long educational copy must live in data, not in a placed Blueprint.

Required world setup

  • Correct specimen on the left and Incorrect specimen on the right.
  • Identical transforms, lighting, camera distance, playback state, and shared dependencies except for the named controlled variable.
  • A/B toggle plus a synchronized playback or rotating-light control when useful.
  • Reset restores the Incorrect specimen and the initial camera/test state.
  • Labels say CORRECT and INVESTIGATE; avoid putting the answer in an asset or world label visible to students.

Required interaction popup

The first view is spoiler-free and contains:

  1. Observe — the visible symptom and comparison instruction.
  2. Investigate — two or three places to inspect, without naming the answer.
  3. Your task — the smallest repair and what must remain unchanged.
  4. Success — explicit validation criteria and evidence.
  5. Why it matters — one production consequence.

The popup also exposes OPEN FULL BRIEF, LEARN MORE, and a visually separated SHOW EXPLANATION action. The explanation contains Cause, Correction, Prevention, and an intentional-use exception.

EX22 — Animation Loop Continuity

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish a loop-boundary discontinuity from playback rate, frame rate, or skeleton incompatibility.
  • Inspect first/last pose, clip range, root transform, and sequence settings.
  • Repair the authoritative clip or export range and validate several loops.

Controlled comparison

Property Correct Incorrect
Skeletal mesh, skeleton, material Shared Shared
Playback rate and looping Shared Shared
Source SK_CharacterMedium_Correct.mb / run.fbx SK_CharacterMedium_Incorrect_Animation.mb / run_INCORRECT_animation.fbx
Controlled variable First and last poses form a continuous cycle End-to-start pose or root discontinuity creates one visible pop

Implementation plan

  1. Import both sequences against the same existing skeleton.
  2. Place synchronized actors and add pause, 0.25x speed, and scrub-boundary controls.
  3. Confirm that playback rate and the skin mesh are identical.
  4. Verify three uninterrupted loops and record the exact faulty frames before publishing the explanation.

Popup outline

  • Observe: "Both characters loop the same run. One has a visible pop at the boundary. Watch three cycles and identify exactly when continuity breaks."
  • Investigate: "Compare the first and last poses, exported frame range, root transform, and sequence settings. Which evidence rules out playback speed?"
  • Your task: "Restore a continuous cycle without changing the mesh, skeleton, material, or playback rate."
  • Success: "No visible pose or root jump across three loops; duration and unaffected animation channels remain consistent."
  • Why it matters: "A one-frame source discontinuity becomes a repeated runtime defect in locomotion, crowds, and ambient animation."
  • Explanation: The loop samples do not meet at the boundary. Correct the source keys or exported range, then reimport over a stable target. Prevent with a first/last-pose comparison or loop validator. A discontinuity is intentional only for a non-looping action or authored transition.

EX25 — Skin Weights: One Bad Influence

Learning objectives

  • Separate deformation failure from animation-clip failure.
  • Use weight visualization and influence inspection to locate a bad region.
  • Make a localized weight correction while preserving hierarchy and motion.

Controlled comparison

Property Correct Incorrect
Skeleton, clip, material, playback Shared Shared
Source SK_CharacterMedium_Correct.mb SK_CharacterMedium_Incorrect_Skinning.mb
FBX Correct character/run baseline run_INCORRECT_skinweights.fbx
Controlled variable Stable normalized weights One localized vertex region has an implausible influence or weight distribution

Implementation plan

  1. Choose one high-motion joint region where the fault reads clearly.
  2. Export the Correct and Incorrect mesh with the same skeleton and run clip.
  3. Add synchronized playback and slow-motion controls.
  4. Confirm the animation curves are identical; document affected vertices, joint, maximum influences, and normalization state in instructor notes.

Popup outline

  • Observe: "The same run drives both characters, but one body region collapses, stretches, or follows the wrong joint. Identify the affected region."
  • Investigate: "Compare skeleton identity and animation first. Then inspect skin influences, normalization, and weights on the affected vertices."
  • Your task: "Repair only the faulty weights; do not alter the rig or animation."
  • Success: "The region follows the intended joints through the full run, with no new pops and the same skeleton and clip."
  • Why it matters: "Small weighting errors often survive a bind pose and appear only at animation extremes."
  • Explanation: Geometry is valid and the clip is playing; incorrect vertex influences create the deformation. Reassign and normalize the localized weights. Prevent with influence-limit, normalization, and pose-sweep checks. Unusual weights are valid when deliberate deformation tests prove the result.

EX21 — Morph Targets: Animation Without Deformation

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish animation playback from morph-target deformation.
  • Inspect a Skeletal Mesh for morph targets and an Animation Sequence for morph curves.
  • Identify the FBX setting that preserves blend shapes and validate reimport.

Controlled comparison

Property Correct Incorrect
Skeleton, animation, material, playback Shared Shared
Controlled variable Morph targets present; Blend Shapes exported Morph targets absent; Blend Shapes omitted
Visible result Cube changes shape Animation plays but cube remains visually static

Implementation plan

  1. Keep the existing display and shared assets.
  2. Save MorphCube_Working.ma with base, targets, blendShape node, skeleton, and animation.
  3. Export both variants from that scene and clean-machine test the round trip.
  4. Add a morph-target inventory readout or explanation image to the full brief.

Popup outline

  • Observe: "Both actors report animation playback. Only one changes shape. What data difference could allow playback without visible deformation?"
  • Investigate: "Compare skeleton, sequence, morph curves, and each mesh's morph target list. Keep material and playback as controls."
  • Your task: "Restore the missing deformation payload and reimport without replacing the shared animation."
  • Success: "Expected targets appear and the shared sequence drives the same deformation as Correct."
  • Why it matters: "A valid animation asset cannot drive deformation data that never reached the runtime mesh."
  • Explanation: Blend-shape data was omitted from the Incorrect FBX. Enable Blend Shapes under Deformed Models and reimport. Prevent by validating animation morph-curve names against mesh target names. Omission is intentional when a mesh is not meant to support facial or shape deformation.

EX23 — Color Texture vs Data Texture

Learning objectives

  • Classify display-color textures separately from numeric material data.
  • Inspect packed channels, sRGB, compression, sampler type, and material wiring.
  • Explain how color-space conversion changes roughness/metallic response.

Controlled comparison

Property Correct Incorrect
Mesh, pixels, UVs, material logic, light Shared Shared
Controlled variable Packed metallic/roughness data sampled as linear data Duplicate texture sampled with sRGB enabled
Visible result Plausible metal and roughness Gloss, dullness, or metallic response is numerically distorted

Implementation plan

  1. Duplicate the imported Lantern packed-data texture; do not edit pixels.
  2. Configure one copy correctly and toggle only sRGB on the Incorrect copy.
  3. Use material instances with identical parameters and a shared rotating light.
  4. Capture channel views and sampled values for the collapsed explanation.

Popup outline

  • Observe: "Both lanterns use identical texture pixels, yet their highlights and metal response differ. Compare them as the light moves."
  • Investigate: "Inspect individual channels, sRGB, compression, sampler type, and the material inputs that consume the texture."
  • Your task: "Make Unreal interpret the packed channels as numeric material data without changing the source image."
  • Success: "Lighting matches Correct under the same light and the map remains connected to the same material inputs."
  • Why it matters: "Import metadata can corrupt physically based response even when the source pixels and shader graph look correct."
  • Explanation: sRGB decoding is appropriate for display color but changes the numeric values stored in a packed data map. Disable sRGB and use a compatible data sampler/compression path. Prevent with suffix-driven import rules. sRGB is intentional for base color and other authored display-color textures.

EX24 — Normal Maps Are Direction Data

Learning objectives

  • Recognize a tangent-space normal map as encoded direction data.
  • Inspect compression, sRGB, sampler type, and channel convention.
  • Validate normals under changing light rather than from a single still view.

Controlled comparison

Property Correct Incorrect
Mesh, pixels, UVs, material, tangent basis, light Shared Shared
Controlled variable Normal Map compression with sRGB disabled Default Color compression with sRGB enabled
Visible result Stable small-scale lighting detail Weak, distorted, or biased lighting detail

Implementation plan

  1. Duplicate the Lantern normal texture and change only its texture settings.
  2. Use matched material instances and one rotating point or directional light.
  3. Keep green-channel flipping out of version one.
  4. Verify texture sampler compatibility and capture one close-up comparison.

Popup outline

  • Observe: "The silhouettes match, but surface detail reacts differently as the light moves. Describe the lighting error rather than the texture's color."
  • Investigate: "Inspect texture classification, sRGB, compression, sampler type, and whether both specimens share the same tangent basis."
  • Your task: "Restore correct direction-data interpretation without editing the pixels or mesh."
  • Success: "The moving highlight and recessed/raised detail match Correct from multiple light angles."
  • Why it matters: "Normal maps alter lighting vectors; treating them as pictures produces plausible-looking but incorrect shading."
  • Explanation: The Incorrect asset uses color-texture processing. Select Normal Map compression, disable sRGB, and use the normal sampler. Prevent with naming and automated texture classification. Color processing is intentional only when the image is displayed as color, not used as a lighting normal.

EX29 — UV Coordinate Contract

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish texture pixels, UV coordinates, material assignment, and importer behavior.
  • Locate one faulty UV shell and describe orientation and texel density evidence.
  • Repair UV data while preserving geometry, transforms, and material identity.

Controlled comparison

Property Correct Incorrect
Geometry, material, texture, transform, import settings Shared Shared
Controlled variable Diagnostic UV shell matches reference One shell is mirrored or rotated; choose exactly one fault
Visible result Labels and checker orientation read correctly One labeled region reads backward or rotated

Implementation plan

  1. Preserve the current Khronos import as the visual reference.
  2. Convert one clearly labeled panel into a Maya scene and export Correct FBX.
  3. Duplicate the scene, mirror one UV shell, and export Incorrect FBX.
  4. Import both with one shared Unreal material and validate orientation, borders, density, pivot, and actor scale.

Popup outline

  • Observe: "One region reads backward even though both meshes use the same image and material. Identify the affected region and what remains identical."
  • Investigate: "Compare the source image, material coordinate input, UV channel, and shell orientation. Which stage owns the mismatch?"
  • Your task: "Repair the affected UV shell only; preserve geometry, pivot, transforms, and material assignment."
  • Success: "Labels, borders, orientation, and checker density match Correct after reimport."
  • Why it matters: "A texture cannot define where it lands; UV coordinates and material sampling complete that contract."
  • Explanation: The image and material are shared; one UV shell is mirrored. Restore shell orientation and reimport. Prevent with diagnostic textures, orientation marks, and UV validators. Mirroring is intentional when the art and tangent/material contract explicitly permit symmetrical reuse.

EX30 — Geometry Autopsy

Learning objectives

  • Differentiate reversed normals from non-manifold or duplicate geometry.
  • Use Maya face-normal display and Cleanup evidence before editing.
  • Repair a localized fault while preserving UVs, material IDs, scale, and shape.

Controlled comparison

Property Correct Incorrect
Source prop One selected Factory Kit panel or box Duplicate of the same prop
Controlled variable Outward, unlocked normals One reversed face; reserve non-manifold topology for a later round
Visible result Stable one-sided shading One face disappears or shades inward from the test angle

Implementation plan

  1. Select a simple panel with an obvious front/back under one-sided rendering.
  2. Create Correct and Incorrect in one Maya scene; reverse one face only.
  3. Import with identical settings and a one-sided neutral material.
  4. Record face count, UV set, material slots, bounds, and cleanup result as invariants; build a separate non-manifold variant only after launch.

Popup outline

  • Observe: "One small region disappears or shades inward while the silhouette and material remain the same. Describe where the failure occurs."
  • Investigate: "Check face orientation and normals before searching for missing materials. Use a targeted Cleanup test to rule out topology errors."
  • Your task: "Repair the defective face orientation and preserve all unaffected asset data."
  • Success: "Outward shading matches Correct; the targeted test passes; UVs, materials, bounds, and scale do not change."
  • Why it matters: "Geometry symptoms can look like material bugs, and broad cleanup operations can damage data that was already correct."
  • Explanation: One face has reversed orientation. Reverse/conform the targeted face and validate in a one-sided material. Prevent with normal overlays, cleanup presets, and export validation. Inward faces are intentional for interior shells when their orientation is part of the asset contract.

EX31 — Modular Asset Contract

Learning objectives

  • Treat pivot, dimensions, transforms, grid interval, and material slots as a modular asset contract.
  • Use bounds and snapping behavior as evidence, not visual approximation.
  • Correct the authoritative Maya pivot while preserving mesh dimensions.

Controlled comparison

Property Correct Incorrect
Source prop One straight Factory Kit conveyor or catwalk module Duplicate of the same module
Controlled variable Pivot sits on the documented snap corner/plane Pivot is offset by a visible non-grid amount
Visible result Rotation and placement snap into the kit Mesh gaps or orbits when rotated/snapped

Implementation plan

  1. Choose a straight module with obvious neighbor connections and document its dimensions, grid, forward/up axes, pivot rule, material slots, and scale.
  2. Create the Incorrect version by offsetting only the source pivot.
  3. Build a three-piece snap jig and a 90-degree rotation test in Unreal.
  4. Validate actor scale 1, unchanged bounds, material assignment, seam closure, and rotation behavior.

Popup outline

  • Observe: "Both modules look identical alone. One creates a gap or orbits when snapped and rotated in the assembly jig."
  • Investigate: "Compare pivot position, bounds, actor scale, grid interval, forward/up axes, and mesh dimensions."
  • Your task: "Restore the documented source pivot without reshaping the mesh or compensating with an actor offset."
  • Success: "The module snaps flush in three placements, rotates predictably at 90 degrees, and remains at actor scale 1."
  • Why it matters: "Modular modeling quality is measured by repeatable assembly, not by how convincing one isolated mesh looks."
  • Explanation: The Incorrect mesh has an offset source pivot. Place the pivot on the contract plane/corner, freeze only the intended source state, export, and reimport. Prevent with pivot/bounds batch validation and a snap-jig test. Centered pivots are valid when the assembly contract calls for centered rotation rather than edge snapping.

Launch acceptance gate

An exhibit is launch-ready only when:

  • the one controlled variable is documented and visually reproducible;
  • Correct and Incorrect use the same test state and unaffected dependencies;
  • the spoiler-free popup, full brief, explanation, and Learn More link exist;
  • Reset works after interaction and after a student reimport;
  • required evidence and success criteria are observable;
  • instructor notes identify the source asset, repair owner, and known exception;
  • a clean-machine Unreal test passes; and
  • DCC repair exhibits also pass a clean-machine Maya-to-Unreal round trip.
