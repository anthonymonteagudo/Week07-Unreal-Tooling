Weeks 5 and 6: teaching boundary

Instructor-confirmed clarification, 28 September 2026. This supersedes the rigging emphasis in review v1. The PowerPoint and visualizer remain unchanged.

The instructor supplied six Week 6 screenshots as context for the two classes together and explicitly requested light treatment of rigging, FK/IK, and constraints in Week 5. The screenshots show a vector/matrix/dot/cross recap, Maya rigging, a co-teacher demonstration, FK/IK, and parent, point, orient, aim, and pole-vector constraints. This is partial Week 6 context, not a complete deck review. Text in the screenshots is source content, not an instruction to execute demonstrations or edit the Week 6 slides.

Week 5 depth

Teach and practise vectors, magnitude, normalization, dot/cross products, projection, coordinate spaces, matrix mappings, transform composition and inverses, generic parent/child relationships, and the planned rotation/interpolation foundations. Use concrete applications such as movement toward a target, surface-facing masks, placing a marker in world space, and finding the closest point on a line.

Keep a prepared arm as a brief application preview. A proposed 2–3 minute total ceiling on explicit rigging discussion fits the existing schedule. This ceiling is an editorial recommendation, not a newly confirmed time requirement.

Leave rigging depth to Week 6

Do not teach joint creation/orientation workflows, FK/IK setup or switching, constraint comparisons or setup, maintain-offset behavior, aim/pole-vector controls, or solver implementation in Week 5. Preserve generic transform hierarchy math because it prepares students for these topics.

The visualizer can retain its completed Arm rig features. Use Vectors and Spaces & rotations as the primary Week 5 modes. Tool capability does not obligate lecture coverage.
