Go deeper by mechanism

Shader programming: The Book of Shaders introduces the idea through approachable examples; its uniforms chapter has a live time-driven result. LearnOpenGL: Shaders connects the concept to an explicit graphics-programming workflow. Their GLSL examples are conceptual/programming resources, not UE editor instructions.

Texture sampling: PBRT, fourth edition develops image filtering in depth. Use it after a UV/mip example is familiar. Heckbert's Survey of Texture Mapping provides a historical research overview.

Surface models: Burley's Disney 2012 notes include parameter comparisons; start with one row rather than the whole model. Karis's 2013 UE4 shading notes are historical engine-model context, not a current UE5 settings reference.

Deformation: Kavan et al., Skinning with Dual Quaternions illustrates why the deformation method can contribute to artifacts. Use the comparison first and the derivation later.

Read a source actively

Write the question before opening the reference. Identify the source's software version or model assumptions, then turn one explanation into a test on your example. A cited technique does not by itself prove that your asset uses it or that your repair worked.

For wider exploration, Bartosz Ciechanowski's Lights and Shadows offers interactive explanations in a first-principles style. Its full lighting scope belongs beyond the short Week 2 introduction.
